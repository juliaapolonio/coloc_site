How to use it

Each page shows the same colocalization results from a different angle, and every panel is interactive.

  • Manhattan — the genome-wide association plot. Hover over any variant of a locus that colocalized and every variant of that locus turns red, while the box lists the genes and datasets behind the signal; double-click clears the highlight, and the zoom tool pulls apart labels in crowded regions.
  • Colocalization Explorer — every test as a bubble, sized by PP.H4 and coloured by whether it passed the 0.8 threshold, with filters for locus and dataset family. Click a significant bubble and its regional association plot opens right below the chart. Start with one to three loci selected: the axis is only legible at that scale.
  • Development vs Adult — one point per locus–gene pair, its best PP.H4 in adult datasets against its best in fetal datasets, so anything above the diagonal is stronger in development. Click a point with developmental signal and a locus panel opens underneath, stacking the local Manhattan, the colocalization lead variant, fetal-cortex PLAC-seq arcs and chromatin tracks by cell type, and the gene models — all on one shared hg38 axis, so variant, contacts and target gene line up vertically.
  • Cell Type Specificity — PP.H4 of each pair across the seven SingleBrain cell types, for pairs significant in at least one of them. An asterisk marks the cells above threshold, so a row carrying a single asterisk is a signal that only one cell type sees.
  • Locus Table — one row per locus: the gene it was attributed to, the mechanism, the cell type and the developmental context. Searchable, sortable and exportable.
  • Previous Findings — where these results agree with colocalizations already reported by the BigBrain, Bellenguez and SingleBrain studies, and which loci carry a gene attribution for the first time here.

Overview

Where every tested locus landed. The outer ring splits the loci into those with no colocalizing gene and those with at least one, and the colocalized ones by whether the gene had already been reported by a previous study. Loci without colocalization open into two further rings: the minor allele frequency of their lead variant (rare below 1% MAF, taken from the consensus summary statistics), and whether its most severe consequence is coding. Click a slice to zoom in.

What kind of evidence supports each colocalizing pair. The inner ring is the molecular layer that carries the signal (expression, splicing, or both) and each slice opens into the developmental stage where the pair was significant. Read a pair as fetal-specific when it appears under “Development only”.

The splicing branch, junction by junction. Each colocalizing junction is counted once and classified by how its two ends compare with annotated splice sites: canonical when the junction reproduces an annotated intron exactly, and the remaining classes when both ends are annotated but belong to different introns (exon skipping), when only one end is annotated (alternative splice site), or when neither is (novel cryptic junction).

A peek at the methodology

The results shown correspond to Bayesian colocalization (coloc.abf, PP.H4 > 0.8) between the consensus AD GWAS (2026) and 23 xQTL datasets — BigBrain (adult, eQTL/sQTL), DevBrain (fetal, eQTL/sQTL), SingleBrain (7 major cell types) and GTEx (10 brain regions, skeletal muscle and whole blood). Colocalization windows were defined as 500 kb centered at the reported lead variant of each locus. Full methods will be published soon in a paper. Pipeline: nf_coloc.
